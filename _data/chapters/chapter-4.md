“It’s *delightful*, isn’t it?”

A melodious voice, punctuated with a thrust of an ecstatic man's arm stirred through the thick fog which simultaneously blanketed beneath their feet all over town. 

Farturn’s town was steadily receding from view from the unusually aquamarine-hued fog, lapping at every structure visible. It had been a war for both cloud and sun for triumph over weather, and the sun finally yielded. 

These thicker, wadded up clouds began to roll in and drift along more of the mysterious mist with, fracturing what remained of the late afternoon light.

As per Farturn's reputation, alas. It had been known for sudden weather changes, which usually favored the rain.

The air bore no scent worth note for now, lacking the steam of a perfectly prepared range of meat, to that of tobacco and industrial coal harbors puffing out black exhaust. Only that of whispers of damp trees were present, and a promise of rain on the horizon, were you one of the select few who had a keen sense for that.

“A *spectacle.* The *rain~*.”

Another man approached the first whom he’d been addressed by, uneasily sliding one foot across the thin beam to shuffle close enough to uphold the conversation's continuation. Carefully, his arms grasped at anything to aid his steady. 

“Soon to be, of course.”

The man behind the one who spoke—when he’d been tipped off this… rendezvous, if you will, he understood little why they spied over the town from scaffolding of a building—the likes of which was under heavy maintenance. 

“Days like these thee acquaints with the likes of himself.” He responds smoothly, regardless of these thoughts that ponder like wild steeds among fields. From this tortuous cycle he hardly could sleep all morning.

As it turns out, proper footing along rickety beams eroded by age and miserable weather proved to be an assiduous task. It swayed and groaned from every twitch that stemmed from either parties, and the second man’s instinct shouted to seize up at the slightest hint of tipping over. 

He held tight—and the other, did not.

*Comprehending my possible death here seemed entirely unlikely at first..* He thought, estranged to the chance.

“Not the rain.” He adds, however hushed. His limbs begin to tremble from over-exertion, combining with his voice, of which, his timbre rolls over like wheels to gravel. Normally his posh accent would be cleanly sorted and authoritarian, however now it only lapped at the edges were someone to pay close attention.

Worser still, exhaustion puddled in abnormal dark hues along the undersides of his eyes, and he looked the least bit approachable. 

“Is that so, dearest Savi?” Within an instant, his face appeared mere centimeters apart from Savi’s, jeering with that melodic tone. The one spoken to nearly launched off from startlement, gasping sharply as he regained his footing.

“Dear god.” Grasping at his chest, he cast his eyes away as the fellow before him slid back to his previous post—suddenly grabbing a pole jutting from underneath his feet and off-shooting to his right, thrusting his weight against it as he leaned the opposite direction.

“I… yes,” Savi decided, his fingers tracking the deceleration of his heart. “I suppose, yes..” Though the sentence left him, he remained detached from the conversation, his mind trampling on elsewhere, though through effort did he attempt to reel it in, or the journey here had been for naught.

Now, Savi had always known his fellow… *acquaintance*, were he to peg such an obtuse name, to be an enigma. Sporadic, if you will, but none the less an enigma.

With his strange brown noble kontusz branded with the suits of cards from poker overlaying a fat strip of white encrusted with gold—a heart suit over the left side of his chest, a club on the other side. Pinning the sleeve holes starting at the shoulder were the clover on his left and the diamond on his right, the sleeves long ends of black fluff. The rest of the kontusz ended in a tail coat, insides bleached red, crested with gold embellishments at the tips, teardrops of lavender clung to the ends of the coat. He bore a vest and another shirt underneath, a fastened white tie along his neck. Along his sleeves were two belts each, ending in white gloves—boots of white with diamonds of gold zigzagging up his legs to meet black pants rolled up to the thigh, a strange rope of colored handkerchiefs of green and yellows resting within his left pocket.

Every *fiber* sewn into the man’s skin was radiating with the lust for disobedience—to rebel, to revolt.. to sew the fibers of destruction amidst the holes society sunk through.

He was practically brought up to these crafts, a criminal mastermind behind plans that would soon be spun to exaggerated (though one could not dramatize much more when it came to this individual, Savi found) stories for decades to come.

His work upheld no less than legend.. 

And nicknames developed through the ages clung like grime, always murmured in the darker corners of the world most were well off to wise up and avoid… But Savi himself never bothered to learn even one of those exaggerated titles except for one he found on a poster:

***BEWARE THE DEVIL WITH SEVEN HORNS***

The name was inscribed on every scribble of news one could imagine. Radio, to newspaper, to word of mouth, always ending in either \*warning\* or *WANTED*.

But, nonetheless, he thought himself content with the alias he’d been provided: Mr. Vasily Ragovski.

Other than his trademark: split-down-the-middle purple and white hair which remained unkept, a devilish mask etched with an unsettling smile by extra creases and folds around the eyes and cheeks, and a singular glowing pupil from the socket of the masks left eye of which pulsed with light erattically—there was no hint of the man underneath. Every inch remained a mystery, save for his heritage, stemming from old Victorian Russia, assuming from old fables that crossed the sea.

Truth be told, times such as these Savi had spent with him decreased his lust to unveil the face underneath little. As far as he was privy, not a soul knew what lay beyond his mask, but most speculation, while crossed along truths, fell into the snaring jaws of mere fables.

Most knew, rationally, he acted inhuman… a monster, among men.

However still the fables stretched the image thin. Some suspected few brought forth this man from the depths of hell via summoning from a cult, mistaken in their knowledge that he’d be bound by ritual.

Savi didn’t allow himself to mingle within these tales, turning a blind eye.

As easily as one would be swayed from… this *creature’s* carnage.

And lesser human still were those “*misfits*” he kept round for servitude back within his abode. Those dolled-up, vile hooded figures dressed in Victorian frill cascading red and ebony. The very same that oft delivered letters of summons when Savi had been brought up in secluded areas of the town.

He noticed they cared not for any human will nor emotion, for they carried no soul—*Bastards*, as Savi referred to them, creation not by god.

He were certain the fables were wrong here, he was \*sure.\*

Yet still—

Mr. Vasily Ragovski, truly, was the devil. Not mere demon.

So stronger yet, Savi was fueled with intrigue of what lay beneath. Was there to be a joyful man underneath? A mauled face, torn asunder from various traumas? Or were rumors true, that a gnarly, disfigured ogre with fat teeth, tusks (though perhaps unreasonable with the mask), and a gaping maw would meet him?

*Or.. if god were to be so cruel… a face I recognize?* Came the morbid thought he braced against, a shuddering sigh escaping him, which went unnoticed.

Perhaps a glimpse along his features would solve the excruciated mind of Savi, and the puzzle he had so desperately cleaved—for an age he’s slotted theories together again and again in wild, abrasive fashion to make sense of the thing before him, as his human instinct begged to reconcile with another… human.

“Maybe… soon.” Savi wondered aloud, rapidly blinking to the realization of his thoughts turn vocal.

“Hmmm…?” Mr. Vasily Ragovski sounded.

Savi remained still for clusters of seconds, his balance teetering in an askew fashion as he watched his acquaintance lean forward in fascination. When no reply stemmed immediately, Savi leaned back gratefully to recede into his mind once more.

*Men like myself who ponder this living danger.. And those silly enough to wager bets until he’s revealed*. He tried to approach closer to peer at the hook of interest that reigned in Mr. Vasily Ragovski, but he’d stepped along his magnificent, broad darkened scarlet cloak (embroidered with delicate spirals of gold) by accident. Swiftly, he swept it out of the way once he gained better footing.

…Nothing had been uttered for a minute or two. Idly, now, Savi bore holes incidentally into the back of Mr. Vasily Ragovski’s head as his thoughts absorbed him. However, all he could see was a brown hood that hardly shone through black neck fur enveloping Mr. Vasily Ragovski’s neck. 

Suddenly so, the man whipped toward Savi, forcing his shoulder to jerk and nearly lose his balance with a startled: *Oh, god—*

Recovering quick, his long black boots thick with traction, he saw what gazed back.

An orange pupil struck him with a leer so sharp Savi winced.

Threaded through his heart, he was sure to show from a glance, was the fear of a man long crossed for death.

The baleful Mr. Vasily Ragovski, just as interested as he’d been in Savi, ebbed away as he turned back to observe something below.

Uneasily Savi clicked a golden button along his chest to close his military-grade, elephant gray colored outfit, five rows neatly leading to his torso, streaked with patterned spikes of red along the fabric.

What felt like a rather excruciating hour, Savi released the tension in his frame, driving a gloved hand in black through his untamed sandy blonde hair.

*That look.. Just as though it were that inexorable…* day.

He allowed himself, dreadfully, to sink back to the places he hated most.

It was… among a battlefield.

Approaching slow and dragging hell beside himself; An unscrupulous monster of a large, grotesque shape crawled from the depths, outlined in ragged, pulsing gold from the fires edges.

“**Your potential is *great***,” Savi recalled the devil speak, as he closed in.

Claws of terror dug and grasped along his intestines whilst he’d been surrounded by slain comrades along his feet. The feeling only spread to his muscles as the being approached, and he’d been rooted traitorously by what he’d thought to have raised a body to crave life. “**I bid you work under my eye, and for that you shall remain as lively as prosperous men shall.**”

"Oh, what thrill, oh what thrill! From this height, could you imagine, my friend?” He whisked his hand into an arc horizontally.

Savi bade no response, unaware he was spoken to.

“...The temptation is *great*–” *(Savi twinged with a stroke of terrible guilt upon the word, slightly present now*) “–far *greater* than I first allowed myself to believe~.”  Mr. Vasily Ragovski hadn’t moved from his prior position, consistent on what he found below. He bore no interest in his surroundings otherwise, bouncing insistently along the pole he clung to.

Rather unfortunately, Savi had been awfully lost to memory to first comprehend what was said, and he fumbled to structure together what Mr. Vasily Ragovski could have meant (*Temptation.. Great… far greater… This height…* Oh).

Following his previous sentence, Mr. Vasily Ragovski’s vacant hand whisked out again, further, as if he were reaching something intangible only visible to his own eye. His outfit drew sharply and made something akin to a zipper being tugged swiftly in response.

“Wonder wishes to see where the fall would take me." His fingers intertwined with grace into a slow clasp of his palm.

*Fall. Fallen troops. Your hand. Slain hundreds.* Raved his thoughts one over another, dashed with blood crowding the edges.

He returned to wander upon the scarlet-and-limb-stained-fields—when he had nothing but a mere rapier (the very one that bumped along his thigh now) and his instinct of survival to shield himself from damnation. He would have been diced upon the ground had it not been what he swore was divine protection that ensured his survival.

Savi muddled his thoughts back to a subdued maroon as he wrenched away—he couldn’t plunge further. 

*God could never want that from one of his children*.

All he could permit the etching of fury lashed into his mind to funnel into the stoke visualization of stabbing the creature before him.

“From there, only Christ could ponder whether you’d truly be vanquished at last.” He replied darkly, almost as if he were reciting it from a page of the Bible—though however up to speculation. Involvement in wars took his mind off such sacred scripts, unallowing him peace to complete the texts wholly. 

Savi glanced back outward, however the creature before him immersed himself in the fascinating item of intrigue far below them, unresponsive. He offered the back of his head the whole of his glare.

Though, this glare was undercut by the fact as it was, well… was *halved*.

As circumstances would have it, he had been condemned to live with a single remaining blue eye that prevailed with the gift of sight.

A day… an argument… a moment, spun out of his control, a man spiraling before him, grief he hadn’t seen before.

*I paid for it. I’m sorry, brother.*

Hesitance drew from the lines in his shoulders, branching into his expression soon after to the present reminder, giving him the taste of copper along his tongue once more. *I’m sorry*.

The wind picked up a little from the altitude they stood along.

Creaks sounded from beneath them, though Savi now remained unworried from its display of  structural integrity, relaxed as they took the mist sinking into their skin and clothes. Or, so would Savi say.

Unable to vouch for the devil who remained unreadable.

“I’ve been much too curious since you denied me then,” Mr. Vasily Ragovski piped up with a hedge to his voice after a long bout of silence, swinging from the pole his left hand grasped—a grip too loose for a man intending to keep his life intact.

He faces Savi again, who shown a flicker of wariness rippling along his features.

Savi pleaded above that the jesters' curiosity wasn’t solely focused on him again—if it were, he’d tear the answers out of him if need be, with bare hands, even. 

*Had there been something I done?* He looked about himself. Having hardly twitched from his place for the past fifteen minutes, there seemed to be nothing noticeably changed about himself.

However, he hadn’t needed to worry long, for the question arose surprisingly fast.

The creature twisted, now, ensuring Savi would see him fully.

“The very scar you brandish there—” Mr. Vasily Ragovski lifts his finger slightly above the right eye along the mask to trace down a jagged line to the corner of the lip, “pray, tell whom hath marked thee so~?”

Naturally, the British man didn’t exactly expect something so out-of-the-blue, entailing him to feel along the side of his face to mime him, his eye(s) wandering.

“From…. brethren. Kin.” He added after a moment, earnest, with a dry swallow that suggested pain lying underneath. However, Savi showed that very little in the contours of his face, the solemn gaze in his eye about the only revealing thing.

“Accidents occur beyond control, on occasion. I…” He’d dropped off, steep emotions meeting his logical train of thought, steering it free of clear explanation, clouding his judgment. Desperately he clung to a face void of tells, but whether he was successful, he remained unsure.

As it was now, it was his old nemesis resentment that met him first, before his saint of forgiveness doused the flame so it wouldn’t stoke higher with a pitcher from god.

But of course, then his bastard old friend regret smothered them both with a blissful curtain of stinging lined all along his stomach. The turmoil of it all could be described as something akin to *hell*.

Savi took it upon himself to shut the lid on the whirlwind that dared kicked up, quick to conceal any hint of the thirst for revenge he didn’t want to succumb to.

He wouldn’t allow himself the lust to manifest a dozen blades to pierce into the one who brung harm down upon him.

That, in and of itself, was unjust, and more than that, cruel—he would *not* be the one to bring his hand down to one of his own. 

Even for the own sake of his thirst of revenge. Even then.

He could not be like that, in the eyes of god, nay. *Never*.

“A marvelous, yet thin tale,” the jester says with a twisted sort of glee that flipped Savi’s stomach into a mess of fury he struggled to swallow—it was as if his thoughts were broadcasted on a radio show. Perhaps his effort to contain his features had been for naught.

To be telling to the devil simply asks for a man's weaknesses to be used against him. 

“For an adequate child with little to his name.”

Clear as day the psychopath beneath the mask took no pity to Savi’s story. Rather, he relished in its misery. “That is, unless you include your precious military status, hm?~”

Savi slid his eyes shut. “I would hardly say that, for a title of a Wolfgang.” The title in his mouth summoned bile along the edges of his throat, and he gulped it down uneasily.

*I shouldn’t have spoken*, whispered a terrible thought amongst his writhing shame.

“My brother… naturally shares this surname.” He reasoned to no-one, disconnected from the very reality that trudged along underneath them; soles of shoes, wails of babes to the clops of horses and discussion along streets, buildings, and all variety of patches of land. Though, since all was shrouded in this strange mist… the sound was all that gave way to the life, along with the specs of amber lights flickering through a weave of all the Victorian architecture.

“I would not bring such past my brother if he found shame where there should be solace in that accursed surname.” He breathed, watching that very breath curl off his lips and dissolve into the air. Until now he hadn’t put thought into the chill that swept along his skin.

“Ah~..." A sound, as light, and as simple as an old man discovering the book he’d been browsing for on a shelf.  "So he leaves..”

Savi perks to this revelation, his nails trying to dig along a metal pipe through black gloves, demanding his body to generate suitable warmth for his current elevation.

“Whom?”

“Oh, nobody of importance~.” There lied a lilt at his voice.

*So he suggests otherwise, does he?*

*What are you doing?*

*Don’t kill the innocent, I can’t bear to watch.*

*Eliminate the threat, you’ve earned this title, now you toss it away?*

Savi groaned, rubbing his forehead with a deft palm. Too many thoughts were toppling over one another to claim higher significance, and he’d rather simmer in the solitude and solace of the fog as of now.

“Oh dearest acquaintance\~” Mr. Vasily Ragovski suddenly spoke, singsong like, “I suggest, as you count yourself the sole proprietor of your own sentience, to stand further back along\~.”

Savi lifted his head after driving his fingers along his eyelids with a snap, zapping vivacity into his limbs to stumble backward, grasping at another anchor to prevent himself from falling off.

“Mr. Vasi–?” He proceeded to ask dumbly, before a burst struck, and Savi, felt a colliding fierce wall of sheer force attempting to knock him back, directed his arms over his head and chest to shield himself from impact, Strikes of *lightning* zipping to and fro along the metal surrounding him, sparks flying off his sleeves and singeing fragments of the fabric that clung to him. Instinctively, his legs locked to brace as he leaned forward to fight the current.

As soon as it came, the lightning had gone. It was when the slam of power suddenly distributed in mass, it was when Savi slowly receded his position of protection.

However, the moment he unshielded his eyes, Mr. Vasily Ragovski was gone.

Fragments of concrete splintered upon strike flinging into the air. Below his feet blanched a wreckage of veins and webs into the sidewalk and roads. No layer of concrete lay between Mr. Vasily Ragovski and the earth, as a small crater had been unearthed by his display.

“Spectacular landing!” He hedged himself verbal award thusly, aloud and confidently said as if he were a radio host, “Exquisite form from Mr. Vasily Ragovski, truly the living spectacle!” He announced to himself, whisking his long sleeves backward as he strode to a particular building of interest.

Flitting along his feet were licks of sparks, his eyes having radiated bright light, though fading now, as he waltzed over to a building, titled:

*Peculiar*, was the single thought that ravaged him, stroking his chin.

Mr. Vasily Ragovski raised his hand, then, parting from his chin—and with a snap, a whirl of sparks transferred from his waist to his fingers. 

Then—

***BOOOM!***

A lightning strike, larger than a motor coach crashed into the front of the building, tore at the seams, incinerating brick, shingles, columns—all that lay between he and what he desired to discover.

Screams sounded from within, not much to Mr. Vasily Ragovski’s surprise, but he relished them all the same with a skip to his step as he entered, shortly after the bolt receded.

What he witnessed before him were decrepit chairs destroyed or alight with small flame and ember.

After that was what used to be a reception desk—a hole gaped through it, as though a beast sunk fangs through a tremendous amount of the wood and tore it away.

And trembling with terror were two girls, staring at his form.

“I humbly ask you excuse this intrusion of mine, ” He tranced over, his towering height certainly immobilizing his ability to converse well. So, as a gentleman were to do, he bent over at the waist to stare them down.

“Alas, certain matters of importance are sure to arise occasionally, would fortune have it such. Might you perhaps, be so kind to assist an elder colleague in a small matter?”

“W–what in *god*’s name \*are you!?”\* Shrieked the first, seemingly a woman of higher class and smoke clinging to her, shielding a girl with a mute colored gown.

“Trifling questions as of now reign little importance.” He speaks with a smile, leaning closer to them.

“Now, shall I be so obliged as for you to furnish me with complete particulars your last patron requested of you~?”

He restored himself to his full height, creaking as though he were some old cabinet door upward and looming all along their inferior frames.

“Or perhaps…” For a moment the walls seemed to expand in contract as he sucked the oxygen from the very space he stood, lifting his hand as a blast of white light rocketed across every reflective surface from his fingertips–revealing voltage that flailed like some wild serpent caught in a net, “more *persuasive* measures might be of necessity, hmm~?”

He crossed his arms with a brisk swing of his head towards the window to watch the bustle again.

*What*? His body lightly buzzed. He thought he recovered quickly, but he couldn’t help the nervous splatter in his stomach that Elijah would nag him with skepticism.

And the horses went *berserk*. Not even a moment of preamble bypassed before their space was upturned in frenzy, sending Zane backwards, knocking his head violently on a wooden frame as a commotion erupted outside, shouts of terror, shock, confusion, and most astutely the carriage man shrieking for his steeds and attempting to apprehend them.

Zane folded forward, gasping for air once his lungs were no longer seized by pressure, following with a fit of coughing spurts.

His threads unspooled out from his neck the moment he relinquished his hold and he hadn’t had the capacity to stop; one by one they stilled both Elijah and he so they wouldn’t sustain any more impacts and smashed into further bits.

“Stop..” He repeated, grabbing at the wrists that shook him, bleeding back to reality via more senses now. He could smell the food spilt all around the carriage, he could make out the dull voices from within the walls, and.. by the devil, was that his breath?

They were traced like a spider's haven all along the inside of the carriage, and they were tightly wound around himself and Elijah, cutting in deep…

Inexorably everything glimmered with a wet sheen—it hadn’t occurred to Zane that it had rained, even for a short amount of time.

Uneasily they settled back on the cushions with the food the wicker basket carried appearing as though it was entirely untouched—which would have unsettled most, had the two not accustomed to it. Zane’s threads receded, as if cheekily, to their work.

“No! I simply.. I.. oh dear.” Elijah hung her head, the rigid form of her shoulders suggesting a sense of an overwhelmed side of her.

“W-we shan’t panic. Perhaps.. e-er… storms here are.. extravagant.. ?”

“*Extravagant*?” Cringed Zane, his bland expression probably unable to get anymore painful than it already had, his lips grimacing.

“...Perhaps I ought to continue no more,” admitted Elijah shamefully, keening her head low, teetering as a man’s teapot would fill ceramic cups.

“Yes, perhaps so,” bitterly rang Zane, as he jostled forward, which he sunk his heel into the floor as the carriage began again its travel.

Zane was none too happy to find himself back at the step of his rental, palming his neck in irritated fashion.

Elijah had gathered the food in both her hands, eyeing the house, with the occasional flicker delivered to Zane.

“Is the carriage man to wait for us?”

“That he’d already agreed to. For he is on our dime for the remainder of the evening as well.”

“Fine enough,” he muttered, his jaw aching terribly from clenching it for a length of time, now. He’d wriggled open the doorknob, jabbing his shoulder into the wood to set it into motion and stepped through, Elijah treading behind him, both vacating their shoes before they stepped further into the house. The wood floor laid a carpet of gentle saturated green, which they tread lightly on.

What Zane had failed to see before yawned to him now; a comfortable scullery complete with a sink, a separate countertop to prepare dishes upon its marble outlooking the brazen outdoors behind them, a drop off hill that led to thickets that were awfully tall—enough so you couldn’t even see so much a glimmer of the town. Though, not as if you were able to regardless, as the sun had paid mind to set many hours early, being early autumn. Beside that, a living room with plush velvet seats encased in exquisite wood carving, and a fireplace, stacked lumber compiling within a grate for later use rested over black stone inlaid in the floor.

To his right, stepping aside from the wall separating the rooms was a dining table, fit for six with a window overshadowing it with glass fogged from the cold, as well as an alcove tucked within the turret that harbored a mahogany desk fit for illustrious studies. Behind that, angling if a man sat directly facing the desk within the turret, a staircase, the walls of it being covered by wooden kitchen cabinets. The railings were wooden, too. A restroom, if Zane were to take a few steps back into the hallway, was further down, along with a pair of two bedrooms.

*Well, that’s about everything,* categorized Zane comfortably, not going about the finer details was fine with him.

Zane, managing to briefly forget that Elijah had been with him, felt his eye twitch when she piped up.

“Quickly, now,” ushered Elijah, Zane nearly throttling her from this continuous barging she insisted on doing any moment Zane went quiet to enjoy something, “no sense in keeping the gentleman waiting.” She lugged her luggage along her shoulder and clambered up the stairs, while Zane, softly sighing, reversed the direction he’d faced and mimicked her, clambering up the stairs into his (temporary) new room.\
