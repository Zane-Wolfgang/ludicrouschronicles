The carriage rolled to a stop at six o’ clock sharp, its residents quickly making way to the restaurant across the slick stone road and sidewalk, sheltering quick amidst the fine blanket of heedy mist that called upon the occasional fat droplet. Here, details were harder to outline in the dark, much of it unrecognizable compared to its sun-lit counterpart in the day. 

Zane merely hoped for Victorian arches of splendor reaching to the skies for this particular restaurant.



He’d also clocked an awning of strict black and white stripes, with a lovely barrage of foliage out the front, lit with glistening gold rimed oil lamps hovering just out of reach of the stalks, shading the glass windows behind them effectively, however doing little to keep out their warm amber lights spilling through every orifice of dark green blades.



***𝑀𝒶 𝒟𝑒𝓂𝑒𝓊𝓇𝑒*** lay in fine print along one of the establishment's windows, which, if Zane hadn’t purposely been on the search for it, would have missed it entirely.

“Is this the place?” He asked Elijah, his right arm above his head, though the glasses didn’t do much a horrendous job at keeping his eyes rain-free.



“Aye,” she said, “and.. m-‘*My Abode*’, it says.”

Zane handed her a skeptical look in response—all he had the energy for currently was reserved for dinner, having eaten nothing all day. “I thought you’d… wonder.” She added timidly.



Zane stayed silent.



“Let’s go-... Erm…” She double backed, catching that he’d stopped to glance at the tirade of rain lashing among the buildings to pay attention to her,” …in?”



Elijah stepped up the dark steps with a coy gesture. Zane responded with nothing to her invite. Rather, he bore his stare into concentrating on his hope to discover the true color of the step below them if he’d thought hard enough, though no such revelations came to light. Even upon the light bleeding onto the first step, which only shrouded it in a hue of vibrant orange, he could vaguely distinguish a light gray stone step, though he remained unsure. 

Continuously he paid little mind after that as they stepped inside, checkered tile at an angle meeting their heels, which clicked upon the surface.

Then blasted were they by the cacophony of floral scents from perfumed flappers and various light spices and potatoes. A dash of meat if you could trace it, and Zane was rather superb in that.

“Golly … these lasses lay it on a bit thick, don’t they?” Grumbled he, swiftly tugging his arm to pinch the bridge of his nose, in search of a waitress to seat them.



A receptionist materialized after discussing something in idle chatter with a co-worker, sliding before them with a barrier of a sleep wooden counter keeping Elijah and Zane at but a small distance.

“How may I help you two this evening?” Came the languid song of the receptionist's voice, soothing to the ear amongst most voices Zane has heard. Perhaps she was more capable of a mothers lullaby.



*The mother I never had…* An intruding thought wandered. Zane prayed ignorance upon it.



There were barricades in the form of small pillars on either side of them, while branching them together towards the entryway was but a small wooden wall. There laid a candle cupped in glass just out of reach of a potted plant that lapped the edges of the wall, severely overgrown; or perhaps that was just the plant, as it behaved more of vines.

Elijah clearly had spoken on behalf of Zane as he tuned back in (he wasn’t quite sure why he was to chalk all these areas so specifically, perhaps something unease him).

“And if you mind, near a window…”

“Yes, of course. Lucky we have just the spot for you two.” The woman ducked beneath the desk and plucked two thick pieces of parchment, letters ingrained on the surface with a large **𝐁𝐄𝐕𝐄𝐑𝐀𝐆𝐄𝐒** imprinted on the back, and what reasonably to assume were labels of various, well, beverages.



They were seated between two windows with one stalk of dark brick between them. Hooked near the end before wood claimed the roof was this scone, per se; it was crafted of glass while holstered to the banister via a circle of wood, and instead of fire, poured from it ferns and little vines. They looked a bit on the drier side, but more or less alive.

*Perhaps this town has more creativity than I initially thought.*

“May we get you started with a beverage of your choice? Sir?”

Zane flicked his head up with a jittery stroke of shock, unexpected to be acknowledged so quickly. “Water,” he spoke blatantly, not bothering to glance at the menu, “with lemon, if possible.”

“No tea?” Elijah asked suddenly, which made Zane feel rather stupid that wasn’t his first choice… being brought up on it, no less, felt a bit… \*hampering\* to his British pride.

“...What tea have you got?” Zane demanded, Elijah’s face flickering between a joy to help but crumpled in how he acted.

“We haven’t got a stock for black or green tea, but we’ve got orange pekoe, blackberry leaves.. And ginger.” No sooner had the waiter finished over his pondering upon his notes he perked his nose high to look down on them curiously.

“Ginger?” Zane twisted his nose in disgust. “Well, fine… no flavors I enjoy apart from orange pekoe.”

“The pekoe it is, then. And you, madam?”

Elijah double checked her menu, flitting over the options until what obscure decision she came to resurfaced.

*Ah,* she spoke softly. “Might I go for a spot of some strawberry cocktail?” Settled Elijah, and off the waiter scribbled madly over his paper.

“Right away. Should be no less than a few minutes.” He whisked off with a nod while Zane keened his head toward the outside, letting the rain consume his thoughts. After all the nagging his threads had contributed to his horrible day, they now reeled in the backs of his mind, letting the throes of his natural thoughts bounce about like birds from branch to branch.



*I ought to tell Elijah about… the ordeal,* thought Zane, inhaling large until his chest swelled, and releasing it with a great woosh of a sigh.



“What’s troubling you, Lord Zane?” She asked deliberately, and he scoffed.

“My ring is gone.” He grumbled, astutely presenting the obvious.

“Oh..! Why, that reminds me—”

“Yes, I.. I can tell you about the girl.”

“What do you know?”

Zane glared at his reflection in the glass out of the corner of his eye, an outline of blue peering over his shirt collar.

“She’s Russian. Seventeen, her birthday is rather close… and, if I remember right… her name… what was it..” Zane cocked his elbow against the bed of his seat, flicking up and down his wrist absently. 

“Rachel Talismen… *Talisman*? And a rather peculiar last name… Redlock, I think it was. Redrock? No, it ought to be Redlock…” Zane’s head teetered down a bit as he receded into his thoughts. Elijah, however, narrowed her eyes, unfolding her provided bistro napkin into her turtleneck through mild shoving. 

Her failure to conceal her anger was hardly lost on Zane, and he was a bit surprised to see her in such a state.

“Here you both are,” Zane jumped as the waiter dissolved into existence with their drinks. Zane’s was piping hot in a mug—he decided not to touch it from the plume of steam wafting off practically the *mug* itself. 

Elijah mustered a curt smile with a small nod, whispering a small thank you as she clasped her fingers around the frail neck of the glass given to her. Her beverage of choice was a peculiar shade of red topped with a slice of strawberry and encrusted with sugar around the edges. It hedged on the color of orange but not quite there. Bloody-orange could’ve also fit the description. The liquid fizzed at the slightest movement, ice clinking a little chime would the movement be too harsh.

“Thank you,” Elijah spoke warmly, with a bit of a sharper undertone than her usually anxious cadence. She tipped her glass upward to the waiter who bowed and veered away to seek other patrons who required his attention. 

The moment he left, dazzling hues of orange ventured into Zane’s vision in a blast of sorts, then spurred in lazy gasps around the ceiling… hanging lanterns, by the looks of it, via chain.  Unsurprisingly, sunglasses in a dark room clashed horribly with Zane’s vision, however much he hated to learn to deal with it. 

And he knew harshly of the custom for it was frowned upon in most places such as these during this hour, but… would he rather some distasteful looks over horrified ones, yes, he believed he did.



“I know what she looks like.”

“As do I, but—”

“Look. The police will hardly lift a finger over this affair.”

Elijah deflated a little to this, trepidatiously sidled with a downcast expression, “then what do you suppose we do? That.. that ring was—”

“—Priceless, I *know*. I’m the ringbearer, for gods sake, I *know*.” 

She hung her head, mildly defeated, the chatter growing to a deafening pitch over some ordeal of a great business prospering further. Zane caught little. He swallowed.



“My only solution is tailing her until I track her down myself.”

“*Yourself*?” Spoke Elijah, aghast, \*paling.\* “You couldn’t possibly—”

“Think about it, Elijah,” he snapped crudely, in no mood to hear consistent, anxious babbling, “if anyone were to locate a girl in a town this large, who would have a greater time other than myself?” He quickly lifted his sunglasses to amass more trust from his secretary, before letting them fall along the bridge of his nose (which landed haphazardly, and he had to right nippily).



She quieted rather quick.

Zane felt his once brisk exhaustion meld heavily with his anger, mixing a most egregious cocktail of the worst possible set of emotions following after the day he had.

His head fell into the offering of the back of his hand, whose elbow was propped on the table, another sigh leaving him.



His eyes found the menu, and he realized he hadn’t even bothered deciding what to order.

“Let’s fetch something and head to the house.” He decided, shoulders sagging.



All he could really think was *father will kill me for this*. A terrible, prominent thought of terror on what would be brought upon him. 

A sneaky thread lifted up and tasted along the hairs of his cheek and he flinched, slapping his face with a bug assumedly on him.

Upon discovery, he snatched the thread and stuffed it back into his shirt and lifting it by the collar when Elijah perked to him.

“Nothing,” he spoke quickly.

She resumed her menu searching.



Zane hooked his finger along the loops of threads over his throat and dragged them down until they weren’t even the slightest bit visible (as they had been peeking over his shirt a bit).

The dirty-blonde followed her example, his eyes skimming the page, earnestly searching for the slightest hint of meat he could find.

And lo, he discovered it, a mighty list of selections all regarding steaks. Roasted vegetables (largely consisting of potatoes, carrots, peas and celery) and a fat sirloin.

He could settle with that.

“Oh—waiter!” Elijah nearly clambered to the man that had just about passed them, to which he back-tracked a few steps posthaste, a smile climbing its way up his cheeks.

“Ah, are you ready to order?”

“Yes, I’ll have…”

Zane committed his choice to memory as he thought he briefly heard her speak about some shrimp.

“And you sir?”

Zane thought a bit too deeply.. “Ah—yes? Me? Oh,” he found himself glancing back at the menu despite what he thought he’d committed to memory well. Perhaps he was much too fondled by stress to…

“Er… um, sorry.” He blinked once or twice, a strange sleepiness apprehending him. “The steak and vegetables, please.”

“How would you like your steak?”

“Er… rare.”

The waiters brow ticked—likely an uncommon choice.

“Your vegetables—smoked or cooked?”

“Cooked is fine.”

“Your meal shall be out shortly.” The waiter nodded, and Zane watched him leave as the chatter of the restaurant and the chime of his thoughts consumed him yet again.

What remained of the afternoon was a blur. He as plenty sure he wolfed down his meal briskly. He managed with a hem of etiquette but his care was barely present. His hunger was far more rampant than he predicted.



They rode the way home in silence, and he swore he dozed off a few times, feeling the nod of his head just before he plunged under, resurfacing briefly each harsh bump.



The rain, as the clouds were all day, was sporadic. Sometimes he heard it through the carriage, sometimes he had not.

When he came to he’d come to the door step of the house, practically pushed from behind by Elijah while his eyes were attempting to glue shut by the eyelash.

He was ushered upstairs—he clambered, and at some point he took off his sunglasses and set them on a table somewhere downstairs.

Elijah seemed to taunt his semi-lucid state as she offered for him to claim one of the three rooms on the second floor that he could hardly figure… no, there was four… wait, was that a bathroo—

She inevitably shoved him to the room directly in front of the stair case, forcing him seven feet forward until he reached the door.

“You’ve no neeed.. To help me, ehhh…” He forgot her name briefly, “*Elijah*.”



You see, he skimmed over the part where he also must remain tediously coherent, though he was sure she didn’t mind.

“My Lord, you are to sleep in your quarters this minute.”



“Yes, yes.. Sleep. Sounds pleasant.” The door slammed behind him, and acutely aware he was that he was suddenly all alone.

*Bed* was the word that blipped to the forefront of his mind, and he wandered to it.



*I should take off my clothes.*

That thought was quickly abandoned as he thought he could lay quickly and settle—foolishly, as soon as his head breached the soft surface of the pillow, he was out.

Zane awoke to a surprising sight.

Instead of the dark corners of his vast bedroom in his fathers mansion, delicately adorned with fine marble, he discovered he was in some small space unfit of his status.

Approximately 15 feet wide, he had himself a desk at the window beside his bed (with enough room to pull and tuck the chair with a resident inside and have enough space to pass between), a dresser near the foot of the bed, nigh directly next to the door if it wasn’t for the wooden frame—though it was flush with it.

A bathroom was to his right. Small, he could see, but he thought he noticed shower curtains—a larger expanse if it was just a small cubicle like Zane had seen in film and read about in stories, so it must guarantee a bathtub.

Morning light filtered seldomly against his feet through the curtain, which drew Zane’s eyes upward. His roof was slightly slanted, the down angle directed to the head of his bed. A few lanterns hung via large bold navy chains.

“Hm.” He huffed, before looking down at himself..

He failed to get dressed.

“Oh for gods sake.” He hastily undid his vest and black shirt, wandering to the bathroom.

A nice vanity met him—while it wasn’t marble, it was of some reliable material that he couldn’t pinpoint in the dark lest he lit a candle, which he didn’t plan to be wasteful of.

He checked himself for what he could in the mirror—his vision in the dark wasn’t the poorest. Though, if you were to ask other folk, they commended him for being able to see well at night. He… never thought much of it.

“No need to shave, then.” Zane determined, feeling along the ridges of his chin with his thumb.



Quickly he submerged his face in a handful of water, then applied soap to his neck (and pinched his threads all the way around to lather them as well) and his underarms, rinsing those free as well.

Then he went about his business, lifting the toilet seat and adjusting his aim before looking about the bathroom, and what he could make out of it.

The current owners of the place certainly adored any semblance of the outside they could muster, with pinecones and branches with some having leaves attached to them. Some were in bowls, others articles of furniture hanging from something. 

A painting sat before him of a girl with a wonderful gown of white, a splendid sight among the grassy background. There were the slightest flicks of paint to suggest movement, for the girl was on a swing of rope and timber.

“They can afford good taste,” he mumbled appreciatively, shaking off any residue before zipping up and flushing.

After last night he could probably afford to wash any possible steak juice off his hands.

To which that, he did.



He went over to the desk to poke around for any possible resources.

Therein lied:

Parchment, a few pieces of. Capsules of ink in the same drawer.

In another, bees wax candles (the scent was near startling, as if they were kept there for a long time without discovery), largely black and white.

There were a pair of keys in yet another drawer.

Anything else was already sprawled on the surface of the intricately carved desk.

A book or two and a quill, a globe of the earth of gold (which were the continents) and black (the ocean). There was also an hourglass… an fascinating choice.

A map was pinned to the wall above it along with a few other notes which looked mildly intriguing.

He could mull over them later.



He failed to notice that he had himself a bookshelf decorated with lines of pine leaves along the shelves. How, he wasn’t sure, but it was there. It crowded one corner of the room beside his dresser, crammed with various novels and other colorful options, an assortment of different charms attached to string or just average bookmark tabs embedded in the books sticking out, or none at all.



Oh, and how did he miss the closet so close to the bathroom? Perhaps he was more tired than he thought…

He opened it—it should have enough room for his case and to hang a few of his clothes.



Good enough.



“Elijah!” He called, checking the drawers of the dresser for any signs of damage or pests. There were no signs, he shut them, convinced.

“ELIJAH!” 

Nothing, his bellow hadn’t received a peep.

He burst out the door and called again, “*Elijah*!”



A short “*down here*!” materialized from below, and Zane thundered down the steps—thank goodness his shoes must have been coerced off by his secretary last night. He’d have hated to already ruin those sheets, because he’d have to \*sleep\* on that grime.



*Though, if they were, I wouldn’t exactly be the one washing them.*



“Elijah, there you are.” 

She appeared to be cooking something with a copper pan, and it released copious amounts of steam—whatever it was, it made Zane’s mouth water. It were probably eggs if he had to guess.

“Oh, Zane!” Elijah lit up to the sight of him—just for her to shriek as she covered her face, tossing the entire copper pan at him.

He leapt back to avoid a burn—his threads zigzagged out immediately to wind around the pans handle mid-air, and collected every single bit of—he guessed correctly, eggs—and dropped them back in the pan. 

“By god's green earth… WHAT was *that* about?!” He shouted, tossing his arms outward as the threads placed the pan back as though nothing had occurred.

Elijah, however, was huddled away, covering her face.

“Please, m’lord… Please put on a shirt..”

Zane looked down.

His eyes wandered back up, accusing.

“Does it *bother* you?” He asked hotly, slightly shamed at the sheer premise that replacing his shirt had slipped his mind.

“Hhh.. M ..my… w-well wouldn’t it be too sudden? Oh dear, oh dear…” Her hips were squirming, and Zane’s shoulders scrunched with slight strokes of discomfort.



“...What in the bloody hell is she talkin’ about…” He murmured, sauntering up the stairs and claiming back his shirt and vest.



Once dressed he clambered back down, Elijah avoiding his gaze, which ticked him off.



He walked past her to look outside more clearly—so it appeared there was a lengthy veranda along the perimeter of the back of the house. That was at least pleasant to know compared to *her*.



“Looks like it’ll rain again,” he commented, it dawning on him precisely why his exhaustion lingered. The sun from earlier must have been but a patch swallowed again by the ever presently interchanging clouds.

He turned and stomped away, sitting at the dining table diagonally across the room and crossing his arms.



“Hurry up.”

“Y-yes of course,” Elijah stammered, her movements becoming hurried as she scratched aggressively at the bottom of the pan to kick up anything sticking to it.

Zane slumped into his arms, sighing audibly again.



“While you wait, s-sir Zane,” she nearly threw her spatula, which she regained control of, “I bought some mail this morning, I ran a few errands.”

“You left?” Mildly surprised, but not bothered. 

“Yes… the residents of the house needed a few things done, and I thought I could occupy my time… the newspaper there behind me..”

She gestured with a flick of her shoulder Zane just barely caught.



But… in no mood to retrieve it, he reeled one of his threads to undulate in slight waves to coil around the rolled paper and lay it before him.



“Will you read it for me, please?” 

Zane grumbled, squeezing his eyes shut before another reserved sigh escaped him and he leaned against the wooden chair, pulling the paper closer to read.

“*Edited by Lucy Grayfodder*,” he proclaimed as some sort of announcement, “*handwritten by the Sawyer brothers, Vincent Wilde and Winston Wilde*.” His eyes drifted down to the declaration of the week. 

“*Date, 10/02/25, Wednesday*.”



A short breath through the nose.



“Headline here, oooh, ***Breaking News***, how enthrallin–… Hey, wait…”

Elijah tilted her head toward him, just a bit to hear him, “yes?”

“...This place.. It looks *familiar*.”

“Hmm?”

“By god, who could’ve done this… ?” Murmured a rather strangely taken aback tone, which was… concerning—with luck it wasn’t major...

Elijah twisted now, intrigued by what could encapsulate his attention so thoroughly—he usually never sounded so… *narrowed in focus.*

“Dear god!” He yelled suddenly, just as Elijah was setting the plates of hashbrowns from potatoes in the pantry, eggs and slices of steak.

“What, what?” She demanded as she trampled over, sliding a plate onto Zane’s coaster, who leaned out of the way. Previously he entirely leaned over the table—she saw how enamoured he was, his eyes wouldn’t leave the page. It eclipsed her curiosity so thoroughly she nearly threw her own plate to settle behind him to see.

“Dear god, it’s Ashoft’s!”

“What? Do you—”

“The place I went to look for the thief’s information yesterday!”



Elijah stilled with shock, half-bent over Zane’s shoulder as he thrust the page before her, a placid murky brown and light yellow image of a smoldering remnant of what used to be a fantastical building, reduced to rubble and carnage, smoking heavily into the sky as though portions had been alight with flame.



“*What*?” Zane couldn’t express a more bewildered tone that Elijah had just managed if he tried.



“Yes, look, it’s got those very steps and everything, the pillars are exactly the same—hold on,” Zane zeroed in on the text, confirming it swiftly. “Yes, yes it is, it *is!* The very same, it’s Ashoft’s! By the sun, look, they say there had been no scheduled destruction… it was a terrorist attack!”

“But how!” She cried helplessly. 

“I haven’t a clue, I …” He tossed the paper aside, nearly nailing his food. He leaned back against his chair, running his hand through his hair and losing a few strands from the action.

“Ashoft’s was *destroyed*. They… they speculate by an \*arsonist?\* No arsonist could’ve done that with just a bit of fire, they’re delirious. Delirious!”  



*That’s too coincidental to my departure….* He thought grimly, his expression hardening as he stared darkly onto the page. *To make the news this swiftly… this must be extremely serious..*



“Yes, it must be.”

For a moment, Zane was convinced Elijah had gone and read his mind until he realized he’d said the last part out loud, though more branded a question.



“There’s warnings of danger all about these paragraphs and to remain wary… it… could be the work of the *devil?* The devil? How…” She trailed off, scrunching her eyelids to the bottom of the page with another capture. 

“And a devilish figure running amok.. Many speculate a disfigured *dragon* of sorts… by god, what is this town gotten into?”

“I thought you’d have done research on this place…” 

“How could I, when your departure was but on a whim? I haven’t *time* to study that.”

“But my *father* told you all about it, didn’t the bloke?” Zane hissed, glowering about his meal. “Where’s the cutlery?”

“O-oh, oh dear, I’ll—one moment.” Elijah skittered off to return with a knife and fork, Zane snorting as his threads took them from her.

“By the land of which the queen rules, *why* does so many terrible events transpire as soon as we land here?”

She stayed quiet, her eyes drawing back to the strange blob with flecks of light yellow along the spine and the smog that clung to the figure in the latter drawing.



Zane ate unhurriedly, chewing slower than he ever would at his dinner table, unable to take his mind off it.

*Destroyed.*

*How?*

Most importantly… *Why…?*



“To town…”

Not again.

“Eh?” He piped up, even if his vocal cords demanded the opposite. Far too much conversation he’d engaged in, he’d prefer some solitude of the mind.

“We ought to head to town and have this trip over with…” Her hand found her other anxiously, rubbing the back of the first, “if your *father* found you left *home*..”

Anger suddenly bubbled to the surface of Zane’s throat, his face blooming into a terrible red. “Who the *fuck* cares what he thinks?”

Elijah gasped sharply, a hand over her chest, “Zane, you musn’t—please, speak not of your father that way!”

“So what? He isn’t *here*.” Snidely, his the corners of his mouth split into a snarl.                               

“I’d spit in his face if I could, anyway.”



He added sharply, his eyes matching this harsh tone of voice.                                                                

“I’m allowed a little damn *luxury*, Elijah. I don’t need to be cooped up in a mansion all my life. All this money I’m earning, to waste—and what’s about all this law I’m learning? For giggles and sharts? That, to waste, too!”

“Sir—” 

“Don’t try an’ *control me too*.” Zane snapped, throwing down his fork. “I ain’t hungry. Store it if you want. You said about the town,” he jabbed his thumb behind him, gesturing towards the door.

“When do we go?”

“A-at… ten. The coach shall arrive at ten.”

“Tch. Fine.”



Zane roamed around Elijah who tilted forward, an air of concern shrouding her as she sulked.



*The last of that is scarcely my problem,* he thought, coming round the bend to look towards the door. No need, their luggage was left. Or, so he thought. He hoisted up his, as Elijah’s was absent and grew determined to pass time as he dragged it with him upstairs, lumbering like a wounded animal.



Somehow his sunglasses found their way into his hand. He must have seized them at some point during his tread through the bottom floor. He disposed of them on his dresser as he arrived upstairs, tossing his luggage violently along his bed and wrecking it open.



He threw what little belongings he bought into places he wouldn’t forget, unable to stop the rampant thought of his.



*Father, father, father this father that why is it always him I hate that man always father Arthur Arthur Arthur why can’t it be me for a change why does he control me even when I’M NOT THERE—*



He slammed the closet so hard one of the hinges shot right off, his thread absently taking care of it as he threw himself onto the bed, groaning loudly.



“Always that damn man… I hope he goes to hell…” He pressed the heels of his palms into his eyelids and curled along his bed, his threads lashing mildly at his neck—he’d done it multiple times, when his fury overtook him—he’d strange himself trying to rip them out. They learned better than he.

Clearly.



Creaks sounded behind him, as timorously, a presence joined him.

“S… sir?”

“Go away.”



Elijah shuffled her feet. Or, Zane presumed, at least.

“.. I will let you know when we shall leave.”

“Fine. *Go. Away.*”

The door creaked closed and Zane gruffly buried his face more firmly into the sheets as he lie belly first into them.

Adjusting his head a bit, he stared glumly at the wooden walls, bark dark, with slitted eyes.

If he noticed, his left eye cast a gentle turquoise glow along the quilt.



*All I have to do is wait.. Splendid… as if I’ve got more time to waste..*



He dragged himself a pillow forward, his steam let off. He felt his stomach surrendering back to the hunger that had plagued him ever since thirty minutes ago, yet he felt no desire for any of his breakfast..



*What time is it, even?* He looked around for a clock, straying on the wall somewhere. Locating none, he went into the desk he plunged a silver pocket watch into and fished for the click of the latch to reveal the face.

“...For gods sake. Eight?” He slumped egregiously on the bed, his posture twisting in the worst possible way to this news.



“Eight… eight,” he moaned, twisting back up, his threads unspooling all along the bed as he straightened out, legs and arms along with.

“My life isn’t about to get worse with this, is it?” He grumbled to no person specific, casting a glance to the dark bathroom.



*Is someone after my head?* He thought. *That was much too aligned… what sort of ‘arsonist’ would need with a place like Ashoft… and just to* destroy *it?*

*And their speculation of whom it could be… a gang of assassins that are running amok town? They’d have destroyed it ages ago if it contained their information.. Not directly after… well,* my *arrival…*

He tossed along the bed, wanting nothing more than to undress and bury himself in the covers properly.



*Though.. About my head… they’d have come for me… directly. A warning?*



He trapeze-d over a few more speculations of his own.

*No, I’m overthinking, aren’t I? Perhaps there just* is *coincidence.*





Something in his gut broiled.

*No.*

*No it isn’t.*



Something was very, very wrong.



*Elijah’s right.* He hated to admit, *we need to get out of here.*



*By the day after tomorrow at the latest…*

*A place this dangerous… I can’t risk it.*

His threads lifted as he raised his hands to stare along the lines of his palms.

*Of course she’s right.* He covered his face. *My damn ‘safety’ above all…*

He scoffed a small *pah* with a cold smile as he covered his eyes.



“Right, *father*?”
